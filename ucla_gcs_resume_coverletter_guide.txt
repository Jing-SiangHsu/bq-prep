================================================================
PART 1: RESUME
================================================================

WHAT IS A RESUME?
- Focused summary of RELEVANT skills and experiences for a specific role
- Read by AI first, then people: ATS (Applicant Tracking System) → HR → Hiring Manager/Committee
- Purpose: to decide WHO TO INTERVIEW
- Average hiring manager spends 7 SECONDS on a first pass

RESUME vs CV
- CV: academics/researchers, unlimited length, complete academic history
- Resume: employers, 1-2 pages, snapshot of most relevant work experiences
- Resume excludes: full publication lists, non-relevant experiences or accomplishments

----------------------------------------------------------------
FORMAT
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Look: standard/boring
- Length: 1 page (rarely 2), margins 0.5–0.75 inches
- Font: Helvetica or Calibri, 10–12pt content, larger for headings/name
- Bold, underlined, italicized: sparingly, for effect only
- Abbreviations/acronyms: ok when previously defined
- Dates: on the right-hand side
- Be consistent: titles, organizations, abbreviations, dates
- Bullets: start with action verbs

----------------------------------------------------------------
COMMON SECTIONS (order = most relevant info first)
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. Contact Information
   - Name, email, LinkedIn, website, GitHub, city, phone

2. Education
   - School & program

3. Experience
   - Reverse chronological (or functional / hybrid)

4. Skills
   - Grouped by themes (Technical, Management, etc.)

5. Role-Relevant Content (pick what applies)
   - Selected Projects
   - Certifications
   - Presentations
   - Publications
   - Leadership/Service
   - Honors & Awards

Example section order for STEM/tech:
  Education > Technical Skills > Professional Experience > Selected Projects

DO NOT include: photo, age, race, sex, height, weight, nationality, marital status

----------------------------------------------------------------
STRATEGY 1: GOOGLE XYZ APPROACH
----------------------------------------------------------------
Format: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]."
  - X = achievement
  - Y = success metric
  - Z = action(s) you took
  - Does not need to be in XYZ order or include all three

Key rule: communicate specific achievements/strengths, NOT generic job responsibilities

Examples:
  Weak:  "Managed 3 social media channels"
  Better: "Managed 3 social media channels and increased follower count by 36%"
  Best:   "Led short-form video development for 3 social channels and increased follower count by 36%"

  Weak:  "Produced weekly reports for clients"
  Better: "Produced weekly reports for clients and increased client satisfaction ratings by 30%"
  Best:   "Created automated client dashboards and support documents, produced weekly reports for clients, and increased client satisfaction ratings by 30%"

----------------------------------------------------------------
STRATEGY 2: QUANTIFYING WORK
----------------------------------------------------------------
Why:
- Average recruiter spends ~7 seconds; ATS spends even less
- Eyes are drawn to digits, especially $ or %
- Concretizes abstract work
- Demonstrates your contribution to organizations

How (elements you can quantify):
- Budget/Money
- People
- Time/Hours

Start keeping records NOW — useful for resumes, cover letters, LinkedIn, interviews.

----------------------------------------------------------------
STRATEGY 3: RESUME TAILORING
----------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1: Identify bullet points/functions/qualifications in the JD your skills speak to
Step 2: Use language FROM the job description to rewrite about your skills
Step 3: Make bullet points achievement-focused and/or quantitative

Example:
  JD requirement: "Ability to effectively communicate complex information to diverse audiences"
  Original:  "Presented at weekly lab meetings and conferences"
  Tailored:  "Communicated research results quarterly to diverse audiences ranging from 10–100+ people for lab meetings, conferences, and public outreach events"

----------------------------------------------------------------
STRATEGY 4: RESUME TAILORING SYSTEM (3-layer approach)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Layer 1: Write a "Comprehensive Resume"
  - All possible experiences, many bullet points, includes achievements outside core responsibilities

Layer 2: Create One Resume Per Role Type
  - Emphasize relevant info, remove irrelevant info, from the comprehensive resume
  - Example: separate teaching resume and marketing resume

Layer 3: Tailor "Type Resumes" to Specific Jobs
  - Review JD for qualifications phrased in different ways (teamwork vs. collaboration)
  - Highlight these keywords in your resume
  - When applying, find these keywords and update your resume

----------------------------------------------------------------
STRATEGY 5: AI TAILORING
----------------------------------------------------------------
Multi-prompt method:
  1. Add job description to the AI tool
  2. Prompt 1: "Identify key skills and keywords recruiters would look for — rank by importance"
  3. Prompt 2: "Revise this resume emphasizing those skills and keywords"
  4. CHECK accuracy and language of results — iterate and edit

Important: always verify AI output for accuracy. Hand-tailoring for roles you're very interested in is also free interview prep.

----------------------------------------------------------------
BULLET SELF-CHECK QUESTIONS
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Does this "answer the prompt" of the job description?
- Does it emphasize my work, its scale, and/or its impact?
- Does it demonstrate what I can do and why it is valuable?

----------------------------------------------------------------
RESUME MISTAKES TO AVOID
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Including irrelevant experiences
- Including irrelevant PARTS of experiences
- Listing job responsibilities instead of contributions (not achievement-focused or quantitative)


================================================================
PART 2: COVER LETTER
================================================================

WHAT IS A COVER LETTER?
An opportunity to "connect the dots" between your experiences and this role using specific examples.

----------------------------------------------------------------
FORMAT
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Length: 1 page
- Margins: 0.5–0.75 inches
- Font: Helvetica or Calibri, 10–12pt
- Use same heading as resume to create cohesive "letterhead"
- Salutation: "Dear Hiring Manager:" or "Dear Hiring Team:" if no contact name
- Close: type your name, optionally include digital signature

----------------------------------------------------------------
STRUCTURE
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. Intro Paragraph
   - State the role you are applying for and how you found it
   - Mention any referrals
   - Close with a thesis statement: 1–3 strengths that make you a strong fit
   - Focus on non-technical skills NOT highlighted in resume

2. Body Paragraph(s) — one per strength named in intro
   - Use a specific example/story to demonstrate success in that strength
   - Use CART framework (see below)
   - Be specific about how you fit the position and organization

3. Closing Paragraph
   - Thank reader for their time
   - Summarize your unique skill set (avoid redundancy)
   - Express eagerness/interest in speaking further
   - Leave a confident last impression

----------------------------------------------------------------
CHOOSING STRENGTHS & STORIES
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Provide context and IN-DEPTH examples of skills, knowledge, experience, and fit
- Focus on 2–3 needs in the JD
- SHOWCASE STRENGTHS NOT EASILY DESCRIBED IN A BULLET POINT
- Emphasize how your skills and experiences align with the role
- Express enthusiasm for the position and organization
- Demonstrate understanding of their mission, vision, needs, and accomplishments

----------------------------------------------------------------
CART STORY FRAMEWORK (for body paragraphs)
----------------------------------------------------------------
For each paragraph, answer each with 1–2 sentences:

C - Context:   What role were you in and what were you working on?
A - Action:    What step(s) did you take to achieve positive results?
R - Result:    How did you positively impact your team, org, or project?
T - Takeaway:  How is this relevant to the role you are applying to?

Example (CART in action):
  "In my role as a Fancy Program Teaching Fellow, I worked closely with a group of 6 other teachers
  with different academic backgrounds. [C]
  Through this collaboration, I developed curricula for remote learning and web/social media content.
  With insight from my colleagues in the ecological sciences, I implemented grade level-appropriate
  interactive activities to demonstrate the relationship between climate change, agriculture, and food
  inequity. [A]
  Through this work, we won the Fancy Program Innovation award. [R]
  My skill in collaborating across disciplines would be invaluable to the deeply interdisciplinary
  research of the Global Social Policy Center." [T]

----------------------------------------------------------------
MODULAR COVER LETTER STRATEGY (efficient approach)
----------------------------------------------------------------
1. Write an extra-long cover letter by reviewing multiple relevant JDs and identifying:
   - 3–5 soft skills common across roles
   - 1–3 hard skills common across roles
   - Write a CART-style body paragraph for each

2. Create a modular template:
   - Write an intro + closing with JOB TITLE, ORGANIZATION, ROLE SOURCE, and KEY TRAITS clearly highlighted as placeholders
   - Mix and match role-relevant body paragraphs
   - Update intro/closing to produce each new cover letter

----------------------------------------------------------------
COVER LETTER MISTAKES TO AVOID
----------------------------------------------------------------
- Being redundant with the resume (cover letter is almost always read AFTER the resume)
- Not introducing new information (this is why AI alone produces bad cover letters)
- Not including specific examples demonstrating strengths, actions, and results
- Not submitting a cover letter at all

----------------------------------------------------------------
INTRO PARAGRAPH EXAMPLE
----------------------------------------------------------------
"Dear Hiring Manager,

I am very excited to apply to the Senior Writer/Editor position for the Global Social Policy Center,
especially after learning more about the role from former GSPC staffer Amy Bruno on LinkedIn.
I am certain that my meticulous attention to detail and my ability to deeply collaborate across
interdisciplinary teams equip me to excel in this role."

Key elements: role name, organization, referral source, 2 key traits as thesis.

CLOSING PARAGRAPH EXAMPLE:
"I appreciate your time in reviewing my application. I believe that the combination of my attention
to detail and my ability to deeply collaborate across interdisciplinary teams would be ideal for
this position. I look forward to meeting with you."
